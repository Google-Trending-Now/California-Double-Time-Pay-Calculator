How This California Overtime and Double Time Calculator Works

Unlike federal law, which only looks at a 40-hour workweek, California calculates overtime on both a daily and a weekly clock. This California overtime and double time calculator applies all three statutory thresholds in the exact order required by the California Division of Labor Standards Enforcement (DLSE).

Pay Tier Multiplier Standard Workday (Days 1–6) 7th Consecutive Workday Weekly Rule
Regular Pay 1.0x First 8 hours worked None (0 straight-time hours) Up to 40 straight-time hours per workweek
Overtime Pay 1.5x Hours over 8, up to 12 hours First 8 hours worked Straight-time hours beyond 40 in a workweek
Double Time Pay 2.0x All hours worked beyond 12 All hours worked beyond 8 Triggered daily or on Day 7, not by weekly total alone

No pyramiding rule: Hours that already qualify for daily overtime (1.5x) or double time (2.0x) are not counted again toward the 40-hour weekly overtime cap. Only regular 1.0x hours accumulate toward 40 hours in a workweek.

Real-World Pay Example: 14-Hour Shift at $22/Hour

Suppose a non-exempt warehouse technician in California earns $22.00 per hour and works a single 14-hour shift on a standard workday (not their 7th consecutive day).

Under California Labor Code § 510, payroll cannot pay all 6 extra hours at 1.5x. Once the shift passes the 12-hour mark, the remaining 2 hours jump to 2.0x double time.

On a standard 8-hour shift at $22/hour, this employee earns $176.00. Working 6 extra hours brings the shift total to $396.00 gross—an effective rate of $28.29 per hour before tax withholdings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common payroll questions California hourly employees, supervisors, and small business owners run into when checking timesheets.

When does double time pay start in California?

Double time (2.0x your regular rate of pay) starts after 12 hours worked in a single workday. It also starts after 8 hours worked on the 7th consecutive workday within the same employer-defined workweek.

How does the 7th consecutive workday rule work?

If you work at least some time on all 7 days of your employer's established 7-day workweek (for example, Monday through Sunday), the 7th day has no regular 1.0x rate. The first 8 hours on Day 7 are paid at 1.5x overtime, and any time past 8 hours is paid at 2.0x double time. Note that working 7 days in a row across two different workweeks does not trigger this rule—all 7 days must fall inside one workweek.

Does this California double time pay calculator include meal break penalties?

No. This California double time pay calculator totals earned hourly wages for hours worked. Under California Labor Code § 226.7, non-exempt employees are owed an uninterrupted, unpaid 30-minute meal period before the end of the 5th hour of work, and a second 30-minute meal period when working more than 10 hours. If an employer fails to provide a compliant meal break, they owe 1 extra hour of pay at your regular rate as a separate premium line on your paycheck.

Are salaried employees eligible for California overtime and double time?

Often yes. Receiving a fixed salary does not automatically make someone exempt from overtime. To qualify as exempt in California, an employee must spend more than half their time on exempt executive, administrative, or professional duties and earn a salary equal to at least twice the state minimum wage for full-time work. Salaried employees who do not meet both tests are non-exempt and must be paid daily overtime and double time.

Do alternative workweek schedules (like four 10-hour days) change double time?

Yes, if your workplace adopted a valid Alternative Workweek Schedule (AWS) through a secret-ballot election filed with the California DIR. Under a valid 4/10 AWS, hours 9 and 10 are paid at regular 1.0x pay instead of 1.5x, hours 11 and 12 are paid at 1.5x, and hours beyond 12 still require 2.0x double time. This calculator uses standard non-AWS California Labor Code § 510 rules.
